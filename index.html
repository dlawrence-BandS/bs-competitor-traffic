How the adjustment is calculated

Similarweb models traffic from a sample of devices, so its numbers are estimates. We have one site where we know the real figure: our own.

  1. Export weekly visits for barkerandstonehouse.co.uk from Similarweb.
  2. Pull our real weekly sessions from GA4 for the same 13 weeks (1 Jul to 23 Sep 2026), using the same Wednesday week starts.
  3. For each week, divide GA4 sessions by the Similarweb estimate. A result of 0.90 means Similarweb overstated our traffic by about 10%.
  4. Take the average across the weeks, then multiply every competitor's Similarweb figure by it.

We round the average up to 0.90, which is the default above. You can change it in the box at the top, and the charts and table update straight away.

Treat adjusted numbers as a guide, not a count. The week-on-week trend and the gap between sites are more reliable than any single figure.

What to bear in mind

  • We assume Similarweb is out by the same proportion for competitors as it is for us. Its estimates tend to be better for larger sites.
  • GA4 misses visitors who decline cookies, so some of the 10% gap may be GA4 undercounting. The true factor could sit closer to 1.
  • The factor drifted down in September (0.69 in the week of 16 Sep). Check it again when we add more weeks.
  • Similarweb data is Worldwide. The competitors tracked only trade in the UK, so we treat it as UK traffic.
  • The latest part-week is left out, because it would look like a collapse in traffic.

How the spend estimate is calculated

Similarweb tells us how many visits come from paid search. We price those visits using what a paid visit costs us.

Spend = visits for the period × adjustment factor × UK share × paid search share × cost per paid visit
  1. Visits: the weekly Similarweb visits for 1 Jul to 30 Sep 2026, added together, then multiplied by the adjustment factor above.
  2. UK share: the share of each site's audience that is in the UK, from Similarweb. We keep only that part.
  3. Paid search share: the share of each site's visits that arrive through paid search, from Similarweb's Marketing Channels report for the same period. This gives the number of paid search visits.
  4. Cost per paid visit: we use £0.72, which you can change in the box above the table. The two figures below explain where it comes from.

£0.53, the cost per click. The average we paid per click on Search, Shopping and Performance Max in Google Ads over the same dates. Our brand search cost £0.16 a click, and our non-brand Shopping and Performance Max campaigns cost £0.70 to £0.79.

£0.91, calibrated on our own site. We ran the method on our own site as a test. Using £0.53 a click came out about 40% below what we actually spent, because Google Ads records about 1.7 clicks for every paid visit Similarweb counts for us. Our real spend divided by Similarweb's paid visits for us is £0.91.

£0.72, the figure we use. Halfway between the two. We keep it below the calibrated figure because some competitors sell categories that are cheaper to bid on than sofas. For our own site we show the actual spend instead of an estimate.

We left out our YouTube and Demand Gen spend, because those are priced per view.

What to bear in mind

  • We assume each competitor pays about what we pay for a paid visit. Bedding and candles, for example, are probably cheaper to bid on than sofas.
  • We assume the paid search share is the same for UK visitors as for all visitors.
  • Some Performance Max traffic may be counted by Similarweb under Display, so spend could be understated.
  • The channel split covers Jul to Sep 2026 only, so the estimate covers that period too.
Source: BigQuery table competitor_traffic.weekly_estimates (Similarweb weekly exports). Weeks start on Wednesdays.
