How the adjustment is calculated

Similarweb models traffic from a sample of devices, so its numbers are estimates. We have one site where we know the real figure: our own.

  1. Export weekly visits for barkerandstonehouse.co.uk from Similarweb.
  2. Pull our real weekly sessions from GA4 for the same 13 weeks (1 Jul to 23 Sep 2026), using the same Wednesday week starts.
  3. For each week, divide GA4 sessions by the Similarweb estimate. A result of 0.90 means Similarweb overstated our traffic by about 10%.
  4. Take the average across the weeks, then multiply every competitor's Similarweb figure by it.

0.92Median factor
0.69 to 1.00Weekly range

We round the average up to 0.90, which is the default above. You can change it in the box at the top, and the charts and table update straight away.

Treat adjusted numbers as a guide, not a count. The week-on-week trend and the gap between sites are more reliable than any single figure.

What to bear in mind

  • We assume Similarweb is out by the same proportion for competitors as it is for us. Its estimates tend to be better for larger sites.
  • GA4 misses visitors who decline cookies, so some of the 10% gap may be GA4 undercounting. The true factor could sit closer to 1.
  • The factor drifted down in September (0.69 in the week of 16 Sep). Check it again when we add more weeks.
  • Similarweb data is Worldwide. The competitors tracked only trade in the UK, so we treat it as UK traffic.
  • The latest part-week is left out, because it would look like a collapse in traffic.
